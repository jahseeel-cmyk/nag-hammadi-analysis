# Marsanes v28 — Causal Grammar Audit

## Primary local sequence

Critical-edition text around NHC X 29*-30* shows a local chain in which:
1. sound configurations are discussed in terms of formal/partial structure;
2. units are commanded to submit / be subjected (Greek ὑποτάσσειν);
3. long/short/dual-time and accentual distinctions are invoked;
4. the execution bundle includes beat, pitch, silence, and hormē/impulse;
5. the consonantal elements are said to call/summon the semivowels (Greek καλεῖν);
6. the semivowels are brought under a single sound / ordered unity.

## Lexical anchors

### καλεῖν
Ordinary Greek semantic range securely includes:
- call;
- summon;
- call to oneself;
- require/demand.

For Marsanes, "summon/recruit" is philologically stronger than vague "associate with."

### ὑποτάσσειν
Ordinary Greek meaning:
- place under;
- subject;
- subordinate;
- bring under order.

This supports reading the passage as an **ordering operation**, not merely a static taxonomy.

### ὁρμή
Ordinary semantic field:
- impulse;
- onset;
- attack;
- forward motion;
- urge;
- initiation of action.

No secure technical ancient acoustic sense has yet been demonstrated for this passage.

## Interpretive consequence

The strongest current reading is that Marsanes encodes a **causal sacred phonological operation** rather than merely listing sound categories.

This remains a reconstruction of syntax/function, but it is grounded in explicit action verbs.

If this chain is genuine, then the ritual logic may be:

**initiation -> patterned execution -> invocation/recruitment -> ordering/unification**
