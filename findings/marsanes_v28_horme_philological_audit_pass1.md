# Marsanes v28 — Hormē Philological Audit, Pass 1

## Result

The lexical evidence supports **impulse / onset / attack / movement into action** as the ordinary semantic field of Greek ὁρμή.

It does **not yet establish** ὁρμή as a technical ancient term for a specific acoustic onset such as a modern "glottal attack."

### Current interpretation hierarchy

**ATTESTED**
- Marsanes names ὁρμή in the same local execution sequence as beat, pitch, and silence.
- The sequence immediately precedes the summoning of the semivowels.

**LEXICALLY SECURE**
- ὁρμή can mean movement forward, onset, attack, impulse, urge, or beginning of action.

**NOT YET ESTABLISHED**
- that ὁρμή has a specialized musical/phonetic technical meaning in this passage;
- that it denotes glottal attack;
- that it denotes breath pressure;
- that it denotes intentional force rather than temporal onset.

## Consequence for the model

Replace the overly narrow gloss:

> HORMĒ = glottal/acoustic attack

with the more disciplined operator:

> HORMĒ = initiation / impulse / onset / directed activation

until a technical parallel is found.

This actually improves the model. It allows the possibility that Marsanes' operator is not merely acoustic, but bridges:
- vocal onset,
- directed action,
- ritual intention,
- or initiation of a phonological transformation.

That broader possibility belongs in the **RECONSTRUCTED / EXPERIMENTAL** lanes, not ATTESTED fact.

## New prediction

If hormē is functioning as an activation operator rather than a purely acoustic parameter, then pre-Marsanes parallels may occur not only in music/phonetics but also in:
- Stoic or Platonic psychology of impulse;
- ritual/theurgic language of activation;
- grammatical descriptions of initiation or generation;
- magical instructions where intention/impulse precedes utterance.
