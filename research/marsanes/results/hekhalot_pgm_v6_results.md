## Hekhalot Rabbati

### HK001 — at least two primary access-control events
SUPPORTED STRONGLY.

A ritual account describes the practitioner passing the entrances of the celestial palaces. At each entrance:
- seven angels are addressed/adjurated by name;
- two seals are presented;
- the angels are pacified;
- an eighth angel escorts the practitioner onward.

Because this structure repeats across palace entrances, the corpus contains multiple primary access-control events.

### HK002 — gate/local specificity
SUPPORTED.

The text differentiates the guardians and seals associated with particular palace entrances, and separately preserves the names/seals of the seventh palace.

### HK003 — operational token
SUPPORTED.

The names and seals change guardian behavior:
- guardian hostility/obstruction is pacified;
- authorized passage follows.

This satisfies the frozen token -> verifier -> behavior change -> passage architecture.

### HK004 — anti-numerology
SUPPORTED.

Even if seven/eight/palace counts are ignored, the mechanism remains:
named guardian + seal + adjuration -> pacification -> passage.

### HK005 — no new operator
SUPPORTED.

The observed procedure fits:
ISSUE/PRESENT TOKEN
-> VERIFY
-> ACCEPT / WITHDRAW
-> OPEN/PASS/ASCEND

No new operator required.

Decision:
5/5 -> STRONG_REPLICATION.

## Greek Magical Papyri control

### PG001 — operational names/formulae
SUPPORTED.

The PGM contain many ritual formulae and voces magicae whose performance is expected to produce concrete ritual effects.

The so-called Mithras Liturgy gives a particularly relevant ascent example:
- invocation and secret divine names are recited;
- an opening petition is made;
- the previously closed heavenly/solar doors are described as opening;
- the initiate sees the divine world and ascends.

Thus strict boundary-opening ritual language is not unique to Hekhalot or Gnostic material.

### PG002 — lower proportion of strict access-control events than Hekhalot
UNSCORABLE AS PREREGISTERED.

The preregistration asked for a proportion, but did not freeze:
- a PGM sampling frame;
- which spells count as denominator;
- how many rituals would be coded.

Therefore a quantitative "lower proportion" cannot be honestly calculated after inspection.

This is recorded as a design flaw, not silently repaired.

### PG003 — ritual-causal formulae without gate authentication
SUPPORTED.

The PGM include numerous healing, erotic, protection, consecration, necromantic, and other spells where formulae cause an effect without an explicit gatekeeper-verifier-passage sequence.

This supports the control distinction:
ritual efficacy is broad;
strict access-control is a narrower subtype.

## Main discovery

The access-control mechanism is NOT uniquely Gnostic.

A stronger formulation is:

Late-antique ritual traditions can encode sacred language as operational credentials, with Hekhalot Rabbati preserving a particularly systematic gate-specific form:
TOKEN
-> GUARDIAN
-> RECOGNITION/PACIFICATION
-> PASSAGE.

The PGM show that ritual words/names can also open cosmic doors and produce ascent, while many other PGM formulae are causal without being gate credentials.

Therefore the distinctive feature is not "magical words have power."

The more discriminating feature is:
LOCAL TOKEN SPECIFICITY + EXPLICIT VERIFIER + BOUNDARY RESPONSE + AUTHORIZED PASSAGE.

## Historical relationship caution

Scholarship documents interaction and shared names/formulae between Hekhalot, Greco-Egyptian magical traditions, and late-antique ascent practices. This means cross-corpus similarity may reflect a connected ritual ecology rather than independent invention.

That does not make the mechanism trivial. It changes the historical question from:
"Did Gnostics invent a secret access code?"

to:
"How widely was credentialized sacred-language ascent technology shared, transformed, and specialized across late-antique ritual traditions?"
