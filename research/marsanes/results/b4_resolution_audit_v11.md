# Marsanes B4 Resolution Audit v11

Date: 2026-09-20

## Research question

What is the most defensible interpretation of Marsanes X 30:12-18?

Competing models:
1. PASSIVE SUBMISSION
2. ACTIVE SUBJECTION / CONTROL
3. GRAMMATICAL-PHONOLOGICAL ORDERING
4. RESONANCE / ATTUNEMENT

## Coptic passage

Repository transcription, X 30:12-18:

30.12 [and] while/as they are altered ...
30.13 ... hypotassein ...
30.14 ... the hidden gods
30.15 through/by
30.16 beat
30.17 pitch
30.18 silence and horme

The crucial Greek loan is hypotassein.

## Critical-edition evidence

The 1981 Nag Hammadi Codices IX and X edition translates the relevant clause:

changed consonants "submit" to the hidden gods
by beat, pitch, silence, and impulse.

But its editorial note explicitly says the passage indicates that the adept who understands the mysteries of language also knows how to chant properly, thereby bringing the gods and angels into subjection.

That note makes the ritual-control interpretation stronger than the English surface translation alone suggests.

The same edition identifies:
- "beat" with a musical/rhythmic ictus concept;
- the next term with musical pitch/sharpness;
- horme as a Greek loan.

## Later Turner translation

John D. Turner's scholar translation renders the same grammatical field more structurally:

- consonants are prepended/appended;
- diverse consonants prefix/suffix the hidden gods;
- this occurs by beat, pitch, silence, and attack;
- the semivowels are summoned and subjected to one sound.

Turner even raises the possibility that the "hidden gods" are graphically unrepresented phenomena of speech.

This later rendering shifts the center of gravity away from a simple devotional "letters obey gods" reading.

## Lexical findings

### HYPOTASSEIN

Greek ὑποτάσσω has active meanings:
- place under;
- assign;
- place behind;
- subject/subdue.

It also supports passive/reflexive senses:
- become subject;
- submit.

Pearson correctly notes that the Coptic syntax does not by itself force one direction.

Conclusion:
AGENCY DIRECTION CANNOT BE FIXED FROM THE LOAN VERB ALONE.

### SILENCE — ⲕⲁⲣⲱϥ

Coptic Dictionary Online:
"silence."

Status:
SECURE.

This is an actual performance/absence-of-utterance variable, not a speculative translation.

### HORME — ϩⲟⲣⲙⲏ / Greek ὁρμή

Lexical range:
- impulse;
- forward onset;
- attack;
- movement;
- cue/timing in Coptic lexical tradition.

Turner's "attack" is especially plausible in a vocal-performance context, where onset/attack is a technical execution parameter.

Preferred operational gloss:
ATTACK / ONSET
with IMPULSE as broader lexical gloss.

### BEAT

The critical edition explicitly associates the term with ictus / beat.

Preferred operational gloss:
RHYTHMIC BEAT / ICTUS.

### PITCH

The critical edition connects the Coptic expression with Greek sharpness/pitch terminology and cites ancient Greek musical writers.

Preferred operational gloss:
PITCH / SHARPNESS OF TONE.

This remains less lexically transparent than "silence" and "horme," but the musical interpretation is editorially well grounded.

## Model comparison

### Model 1 — PASSIVE SUBMISSION

Claim:
changed consonants submit to hidden gods.

Evidence:
- older translation surface wording;
- passive sense of hypotassein is possible.

Problems:
- agency direction is grammatically ambiguous;
- later Turner translation treats the verb structurally as prefix/suffix;
- Pearson's own note interprets the ritual as bringing gods/angels into subjection.

Current status:
POSSIBLE, BUT NOT PREFERRED AS THE SOLE READING.

### Model 2 — ACTIVE SUBJECTION / CONTROL

Claim:
properly performed altered sounds subject/control divine or angelic powers.

Evidence:
- active sense of hypotassein is possible;
- Pearson's editorial note explicitly says proper chanting brings gods and angels into subjection;
- neighboring late-antique ritual traditions give divine names/sounds operative efficacy.

Problems:
- the text itself does not transparently state "the practitioner controls the gods";
- Turner later prefers a grammatical prefix/suffix rendering.

Current status:
PLAUSIBLE AND PHILOLOGICALLY SERIOUS, BUT NOT SECURE.

### Model 3 — GRAMMATICAL-PHONOLOGICAL ORDERING

Claim:
the passage describes how altered consonantal forms are attached/ordered relative to hidden divine or sound phenomena through controlled prosodic execution.

Evidence:
- Turner translates the key verbs as prefix/suffix;
- the whole section is explicitly about grammatical letter classes and nomenclature;
- semivowels are summoned and reduced/subordinated to a single sound;
- "hidden gods" may denote ungraphed/latent speech phenomena in Turner's interpretation.

Current status:
STRONGEST STRICT-PHILOLOGICAL MODEL.

### Model 4 — RESONANCE / ATTUNEMENT

Claim:
beat, pitch, silence, and onset tune the sacred form into correspondence with hidden powers.

Evidence:
- fits the performance bundle;
- fits wider theurgic/sacred-sound comparanda;
- explains why written form alone is insufficient.

Problems:
- "resonance" and "attunement" are interpretive words, not explicit lexical equivalents in the passage.

Current status:
HIGH-VALUE ESOTERIC HYPOTHESIS, NOT DIRECT TRANSLATION.

## Best current synthesis

The strongest conservative reading is:

ALTERED CONSONANTAL FORM
+ RHYTHMIC BEAT
+ PITCH
+ SILENCE
+ ATTACK/ONSET
-> ORDERING / ATTACHMENT / SUBJECTION RELATION
WITH HIDDEN DIVINE OR PHONETIC POWERS.

Agency direction remains partially open.

The most likely ritual implication is not passive pronunciation alone.
The passage appears to describe a controlled performance system in which sacred linguistic forms change function according to how they are executed.

## Project implication

This materially strengthens the PERFORMANCE layer of the Marsanes grammar.

The text's formal system is no longer best modeled as:

LETTER -> MEANING

or even only:

CLASS -> FORM.

The stronger model is:

CLASS
-> GENERATED FORM
-> PERFORMANCE PARAMETERS
-> RELATION TO HIDDEN POWER
-> EFFECT.

That is the first version of the project in which PERFORMANCE is structurally indispensable rather than decorative.
