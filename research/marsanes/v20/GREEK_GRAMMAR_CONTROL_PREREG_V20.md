# Greek Grammar Control Test v20 — PREREGISTRATION

Freeze date: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Test whether Marsanes' special treatment of semivowels can be explained by ordinary ancient Greek grammatical theory, or whether Marsanes adds a distinct sacralized/performance layer.

## Core contrast

Marsanes v19 leading model:

PERFORMANCE
-> ACTIVE SEMIVOWEL SUMMONING
-> ORDERING / UNIFICATION
-> SINGLE SOUND

The new question is:

What part of this architecture already exists in ordinary Greek grammar?

## Primary ancient control sources

1. Dionysius Thrax, Techne Grammatikē
2. ancient scholia/commentary traditions on Dionysius Thrax where accessible
3. later grammatical witnesses only as secondary continuity controls

## Frozen variables

For each source passage code:

G20-1 SEMIVOWEL_CLASS
Does the source explicitly distinguish hēmiphōna / semivowels?

G20-2 PARTIAL_OR_INTERMEDIATE_VOICE
Are semivowels described as:
- less fully voiced,
- intermediate,
- murmuring/hissing,
- incomplete relative to vowels,
- otherwise acoustically special?

G20-3 ACTIVE_OPERATION
Are semivowels said to be:
- summoned,
- called,
- activated,
- subjected/ordered,
- transformed,
- brought into a new sounded state?

Simple classification does NOT count.

G20-4 MANY_TO_ONE
Does the source describe multiple semivowels or phonetic elements being unified into one sound?

G20-5 PERFORMANCE_PARAMETERS
Does the source bind semivowels specifically to:
- beat/ictus,
- pitch,
- silence,
- attack/onset,
- or an equivalent execution bundle?

G20-6 SACRED_BINDING
Are the same phonological classes explicitly bound to:
- gods,
- angels,
- soul configuration,
- ritual nomenclature,
- hidden powers?

## Hypotheses

### H20-A — ORDINARY-GRAMMAR SUBSTRATE
Ancient grammar already treats semivowels as a special/intermediate acoustic class.

Success:
G20-1 and G20-2 supported in at least one pre-/early-imperial grammatical source.

### H20-B — MARSANES PERFORMANCE INNOVATION
Ordinary grammar does NOT use an active-performance transition equivalent to Marsanes.

Success:
control sources support G20-1/G20-2 but NOT G20-3/G20-5.

### H20-C — MARSANES MANY-TO-ONE INNOVATION
Ordinary grammar does NOT bind semivowels to explicit one-sound unification.

Success:
G20-4 absent from controls while present in Marsanes X30.

### H20-D — SACRALIZATION
Ordinary grammar classifies semivowels without the metaphysical bindings found in Marsanes.

Success:
G20-6 absent from grammatical controls.

## Decision rule

If H20-A, B, C, and D all hold:
conclude that Marsanes appropriates an ordinary grammatical distinction and sacralizes/ritualizes its transition mechanics.

If H20-A holds but B/C fail:
the performance/unification layer may be inherited grammatical technique rather than distinctively sacralized.

If H20-A fails:
Marsanes' semivowel emphasis itself becomes more unusual.

## Evidence hierarchy

PRIMARY:
ancient grammatical text / critical translation.

SECONDARY:
peer-reviewed linguistic/history-of-grammar scholarship.

TERTIARY:
modern teaching summaries — background only, not scored.

## Anti-fitting

Do not:
- equate 'semivowel' with modern phonetics automatically;
- infer active ritualization from ordinary words like sound/voice;
- treat later Byzantine definitions as direct evidence for Hellenistic usage unless continuity is explicit;
- count sacred metaphors in a commentator as part of Dionysius Thrax unless textually attached to the grammatical doctrine.
