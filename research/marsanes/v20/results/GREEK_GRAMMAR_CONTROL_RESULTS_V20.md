# Greek Grammar Control Test v20 — RESULTS

Date: 2026-09-21

## Question

Is Marsanes' semivowel emphasis itself unusual, or is the unusual feature what Marsanes DOES with an ordinary Greek grammatical category?

## Primary control result

The control tradition strongly establishes that semivowels were already a technically special phonetic class long before Marsanes.

### Plato, Cratylus 424c

Plato distinguishes:
- vowels;
- mutes/voiceless letters;
- a third group that is neither vowel nor mute.

This gives an early class distinction but does not yet supply Marsanes-style active transition mechanics.

### Plato, Philebus 18b-c

Theuth's analysis separates:
- vowels;
- elements that are not vowels but participate in sound;
- mutes.

Important:
the passage gives the classification a mythic/divine-discovery frame.

But it does NOT:
- summon the semivowels;
- bind them to angels/hidden gods;
- prescribe beat/pitch/silence/hormē.

Therefore mythic origin must not be confused with ritual phonetic operation.

### Aristotle, Poetics 1456b

Aristotle gives a threefold distinction:
- vowel;
- semivowel;
- mute.

The semivowel is acoustically intermediate:
it has audible sound under constrained articulatory/combination conditions, unlike the vowel's fuller independent sound and unlike the mute.

This securely shows that special acoustic status is ordinary Greek theory, not a Marsanes invention.

### Techne Grammatikē attributed to Dionysius Thrax

The text explicitly lists eight hēmiphōna.

It explains their name by their being less fully/easily sounded than vowels and producing hissing/mumbling-type sounds when attempted alone.

It also distinguishes:
- double consonants;
- unchangeable/liquid consonants;
- syllabic combinations.

This is extremely close to Marsanes' taxonomic substrate.

Authorship/date caution:
the extant Techne's precise attribution and dating are disputed in modern scholarship.

Therefore v20 does NOT depend on the Techne alone.

### Dionysius of Halicarnassus, De Compositione 14

This independent first-century BCE witness is decisive.

It says some non-vowels can produce:
- whizzing;
- hissing;
- murmuring-like sounds,

while others cannot sound by themselves.

It further describes semivowels as:
- better pronounced with vowels;
- worse/imperfectly pronounced by themselves.

It then gives detailed articulatory/acoustic descriptions of individual semivowels.

This independently verifies that semivowels were already treated as a partial/intermediate sound class.

## H20-B — MARSANES PERFORMANCE INNOVATION

SUPPORTED in the sampled controls.

None of the control texts:
- summons semivowels as an active class;
- subjects/orders them through a transition operation;
- binds semivowels specifically to beat + pitch + silence + hormē.

This remains true even though ordinary phonetic/rhetorical sources discuss:
- accent/pitch;
- breath;
- articulation;
- duration;
elsewhere.

The distinctive Marsanes feature is the LOCAL BINDING of execution parameters to an active semivowel operation.

## H20-C — MANY-TO-ONE INNOVATION

SUPPORTED, with precision.

Ordinary grammar certainly discusses:
- combining consonants and vowels;
- double consonants composed of multiple elements;
- syllable formation.

But none of the sampled controls gives the specific sequence:

SEMIVOWEL CLASS
-> ACTIVE SUMMONING/ORDERING
-> SINGULAR SOUND RESULT.

Therefore Marsanes' X30 operation is not reproduced by the sampled ordinary grammar controls.

Caution:
this is absence in the sampled controls, not proof that no lost ancient grammatical text ever contained something similar.

## H20-D — SACRALIZATION

SUPPORTED.

The control grammar sources do not bind the semivowel mechanism itself to:
- gods;
- angels;
- soul configurations;
- hidden powers;
- ritual nomenclature.

Plato's Philebus attributes the discovery/classification of letters to Theuth, but this is a divine-origin narrative, not a claim that semivowel execution manipulates or names divine powers.

Marsanes therefore adds a genuine metaphysical binding layer.

## Important correction to the esoteric interpretation

The esoteric novelty is NOT:

"semivowels are mysterious because they are half-voiced."

Ancient Greek grammar already knew that.

The stronger novelty is:

an ordinary intermediate sound class is inserted into a sacred procedure where it is actively summoned and performance-governed.

That is a much more constrained claim.
