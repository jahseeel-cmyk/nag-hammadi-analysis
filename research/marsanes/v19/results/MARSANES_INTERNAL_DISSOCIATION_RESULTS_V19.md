## C19-1 — all semivowel occurrences

### X26:27–X27:4
Semivowels are CLASSIFIED:
- their sounds are superior to the voiceless consonants;
- double consonants are superior to semivowels that do not change.

No action such as summon/call/activate is applied to them.
No performance bundle occurs.

Classification only.

### X30:19–24
Semivowels are acted upon:
- they are summoned/called;
- all are hypotassein to a single sound in Turner's later translation;
- the following line discusses unchanging/double consonants existing among the semivowels.

This is the ONLY secure active-semivowel operation found in the surviving tractate.

Performance bundle is present immediately before it.

## C19-2 — singular acoustic-result language

Two distinct cases matter.

### X30:19–22
Turner:
semivowels are subjected to a SINGLE SOUND.

Coptic:
[ⲥⲙ]ⲏ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲱⲧ

This is the X30 performance case.

Pearson's older translation renders the phrase "with one accord," showing a translation-history ambiguity, but the later Turner rendering takes the Coptic as a singular sound result.

### X31:5–10
The resulting syllables become:
one, two, three ... up to seven
HAVING A SIMPLE SOUND,
contrasted with forms having TWO SOUNDS.

This is a separate consonant-vowel generation operation.

Crucially:
- no semivowel activation verb occurs in this operation;
- no beat/pitch/silence/hormē bundle occurs.

This is the only credible internal D19-B dissociation candidate.

## D19-B — singular/simple-sound result without semivowel activation

FOUND, with an important qualification.

X31:5–10 describes generated syllables as having a SIMPLE SOUND, contrasted with TWO SOUNDS.

The local operation begins with consonant-vowel combination and does not summon semivowels.

No performance bundle appears.

Therefore the frozen D19-B search criterion is satisfied at the level of:
SINGULAR ACOUSTIC RESULT WITHOUT SEMIVOWEL ACTIVATION.

However:
"simple sound" in X31 may be a monophthong/simple-vs-double classification rather than the exact same MANY->ONE reduction as X30.

So it is a valid dissociation under the preregistered lexical criterion,
but not proof that X31 instantiates the identical phonetic operation.

## Preregistration inconsistency discovered

The v19 preregistration contains a conflict:

The D19-B interpretation rule says:
- if one-sound without semivowel is found WITH performance, MANY->ONE gains priority;
- if found WITHOUT performance, MANY->ONE alone is insufficient.

But the later frozen decision summary says:
"If D19-B exists and D19-A does not, favor MANY->ONE."

Those instructions conflict in the actual observed case because D19-B is found WITHOUT performance.

We do NOT silently choose the rule that favors a preferred hypothesis.

Resolution:
report both consequences and treat the more specific D19-B execution-contingency rule as the scientifically relevant diagnostic, while marking the global decision rule as underspecified.

## Main result

The internal dissociation search weakens the claim that a singular/simple sound outcome BY ITSELF triggers performance.

Marsanes can generate a SIMPLE SOUND in X31 without:
- semivowel summoning;
- beat;
- pitch;
- silence;
- hormē.

By contrast, the only active semivowel-summoning passage is X30, and that is exactly where the performance bundle occurs.

Therefore the best current internal discriminator is:

SEMIVOWEL ACTIVATION
rather than
SINGULAR-SOUND OUTPUT ALONE.
